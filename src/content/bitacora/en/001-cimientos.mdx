I've spent a long time thinking about how to learn and grow as a developer. I made plans, outlines, and study roadmaps. In the end I realized something simple: **what works for me is having something to keep feeding.**

A study plan gets abandoned quietly. A public site with a dev log, not so much.

## The rule

Starting today, everything I learn or build has to end up in one of two places:

- **A public repository** at [github.com/tlanexai](https://github.com/tlanexai), with a README explaining what it is and why it exists.
- **An entry in this dev log**, covering what I did, what I learned, and what broke.

That way learning and building the brand stop being two separate tasks.

## Why "Tlanex"

It comes from *tlanextli*, a Nahuatl word meaning radiance, light, clarity. That's what I want my work to do: bring clarity. Code that's easy to understand, processes that are documented, and learning that's shared.

The visual style blends those Mexica roots with a cyberpunk aesthetic: obsidian, jade, gold, and neon, with the stepped fret pattern as a signature.

## The experiment

This month I'm building **without social media**. Just the site, the code, and consistency. So it isn't judged on "how it felt," these are the goals:

- 4 dev log entries published.
- 2 projects with a decent README.
- Commits at least 3 days a week.

If the foundation looks solid by December, the next step is registering the brand with IMPI, Mexico's trademark office.

## What's next

This site is project 001. It's built with Next.js, deployed on Vercel, and its code is public. Everything I add to it will be logged here too.
